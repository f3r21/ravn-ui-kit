## Semantic aliases

Role-based names layered over the ramps above, so components can express intent
(e.g. "this is muted text") instead of a raw ramp position. Every alias points
at an already-verified token — no new hex introduced. Named as bare nouns
(not e.g. `--color-text-main`) because Tailwind v4 generates
`{property}-{token-name}` utilities — prefixing the name with the property
would double up (`text-text-main`) instead of the intended `text-main`.

| Alias                     | Aliases     | Utility                                 | Role                                                                      |
| ------------------------- | ----------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `--color-main`            | `neutral-1` | `text-main`                             | Primary readable text on the dark shell, and icon tint via `currentColor` |
| `--color-muted`           | `neutral-2` | `text-muted` / `placeholder:text-muted` | Secondary/de-emphasized text, dim icons, placeholders                     |
| `--color-interactive`     | `primary-4` | `text-interactive`                      | Active/interactive/hover-clickable text & icons                           |
| `--color-danger`          | `danger-5`  | `text-danger`                           | Error/validation/status-danger text                                       |
| `--color-surface-neutral` | `neutral-1` | `bg-surface-neutral`                    | Light input/card background                                               |
| `--color-subtle`          | `neutral-2` | `border-subtle`                         | Default hairline border                                                   |
| `--color-surface-overlay` | `neutral-3` | `bg-surface-overlay`                    | Floating popover/dialog/modal backgrounds                                 |
| `--color-surface-panel`   | `neutral-4` | `bg-surface-panel`                      | Card/container/panel backgrounds                                          |
| `--color-surface-shell`   | `neutral-5` | `bg-surface-shell`                      | Outermost app-shell / deepest dropdown backgrounds                        |

`--color-muted` and `--color-subtle` both intentionally alias `neutral-2` —
same ramp step, different role depending on which property consumes it; this
is exactly why a role-based layer earns its keep over the raw ramp. The three
`surface-*` depth-tier aliases follow the same pattern one level further:
`surface-neutral` (light, `neutral-1`), `surface-overlay` (`neutral-3`),
`surface-panel` (`neutral-4`), and `surface-shell` (`neutral-5`) each name the
single most consistent background role at that ramp step.

Every `text-main`/`text-muted`/`text-interactive`/`text-danger`/
`bg-surface-neutral`/`border-subtle` call site across the kit now uses these
aliases — a small number of genuinely different-role usages of the same raw
color are deliberately left as raw ramp classes rather than force-fit:
`text-primary-2` on `Tag`'s own `red` accent colour, and on `TaskTable`'s
"overdue" due-date text (a status signal, not interactivity).

`Tag`'s label is a step lighter than its fill, and that is a deliberate
deviation. The design paints label and fill from one swatch — `bg-X/10 text-X`
— which cannot clear AA, because a 10% tint of a colour is never far from that
colour: red measured **2.61:1** on its own tint and green **3.66:1**. No choice
of fill rescues them either, since `primary-4` as text clears 4.5:1 against
nothing in this palette. So the fills stay exactly as drawn and the labels step
up their own ramps to the first rung that clears AA on all three dark surfaces
— `primary-2` (4.98:1) and `secondary-2` (5.50:1). `yellow` is untouched
because `tertiary-4` already cleared it, and `blue` labels in `text-main`
because `--color-blue` is a standalone accent with no lighter step to reach for.

`Avatar` used to be a third such case — `bg-primary-1 text-primary-4` for its
initials fallback — and is no longer. That pairing measured **2.61:1** and was
the largest single accessibility defect in the kit, 46 of 131 axe violations
from one class. Its initials are now `text-neutral-5` on the same tint
(**10.50:1**), the colour this kit already uses for text on a light surface.
There was nothing to weigh against the fix: the design has no initials state
at all, so the pairing was invented rather than transcribed.

`neutral-2` has one more un-aliased role, as a **10%-alpha fill** rather than a
colour in its own right: `bg-neutral-2/10` is the design's chip surface —
`rgba(148, 151, 154, 0.1)` — on `Tag` and on the `Select`/`MultiSelect` triggers
that adopt `Tag`'s treatment. Neither `--color-muted` (text) nor
`--color-subtle` (a border) describes a fill, and `--color-surface-neutral` is
the light one, so this stays on the raw ramp step until the design gives the
tint a name of its own.

Similarly, `neutral-3`/`neutral-4`/`neutral-5` each play other real roles
beyond the one `surface-*` alias above captures — deliberately left
un-aliased since they don't share one coherent name: `neutral-3` as a
hover/selected-state background (`text-button.tsx`, `datepicker-menu.tsx`,
`sidebar-item.tsx`'s badge pill) and as a border (`task-table.tsx`'s cells);
`neutral-4` as a divider border and button-hover background (`modal.tsx`) and
as text (`badge.tsx`); `neutral-5` as body text (`input.tsx`/`datepicker.tsx`)
and as a border (`user-row.tsx`'s status-dot cutout).

This list used to include "`neutral-3` as label text". It was wrong, and it was
the only place in the repo that sanctioned the pairing: `#393D41` on the app
shell measures **1.41:1**, and the Figma exports use it as a background 55 times
and a border 283 times but never once as a `color`. Field labels now use
`--color-main` when visible, and are `sr-only` by default — see
`form-field.tsx`. `contrast.test.ts` asserts the ratios so this cannot drift back.

## Two colour vocabularies, deliberately kept apart

Categorical accent colours and semantic status tones are different systems and
must not be merged — `AccentColor` (`neutral`/`red`/`green`/`yellow`/`blue`)
names _which hue_, on the `Primary`/`Secondary`/`Tertiary` brand ramps and
matching Figma's own `Type` property on the "Tag" component set; `StatusTone`
(`neutral`/`success`/`warning`/`danger`) names _what a state means_, on the
palette's separate `Success`/`Warning`/`Danger` ramps. The two look adjacent
but are not the same values: `success-4` (`#80DA5B`) is not `secondary-4`
(`#70B252`), and `danger-5` (`#E82F39`) is not `primary-4` (`#DA584B`). Use
`Tag` for a category, `Badge` for a status.

`Button`/`TextButton`'s `variant` (`primary`/`secondary`) belongs to neither —
it is Figma's own "Property 1" action-hierarchy axis, where colour is a
consequence rather than the choice. See `src/types/color-variants.ts`.
